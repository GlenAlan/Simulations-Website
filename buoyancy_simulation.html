About this simulation

Draw an object, drop it into a tank of liquid and see whether it floats. Your drawing is a slice through an object 10 cm deep, so every mass and force is in real units.

Key ideas

  • Archimedes' principle: the liquid pushes up with the weight of the liquid the object displaces: Fb = ρliquid g Vsubmerged.
  • Density decides: an object floats if its average density is below the liquid's, with that fraction of it under the surface: pine (500 kg/m³) floats half under water, and steel sinks in water but floats on mercury.
  • Hollow things float: sealed air (hatched) and the air in an open hull add volume but hardly any mass. Once water pours over the rim, the hull fills and sinks.
  • Stability: weight acts at the centre of mass, buoyancy at the centre of the submerged part. If a tilt moves the buoyancy to the low side, the object rights itself.

Things to try

  • Drop the steel boat, then drag one side under until it swamps.
  • Drop a steel block in water, then switch the liquid to mercury.

How to use it

Pick a material and a tool and drag on the tank to draw (a finger works too), then press Drop. Drag the object to lift or throw it, or tap the liquid for waves. opens the settings: liquid, ready-made objects, force arrows and gravity.

The model is 2D: every 1 cm cell is cut along the liquid surface to find exactly what is submerged, and liquid inside a hull keeps a level surface and flows through its openings. Drag and waves are included; swirling flow is not.
