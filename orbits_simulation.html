About this simulation

Every mass pulls on every other mass. This simulation adds up those pulls for every pair of bodies, thousands of times per orbit, in real units: distances in AU (Earth to Sun), times in years, masses in Suns and Earths.

What you're seeing

  • Pink arrows: the gravitational force on each body. The Sun pulls Earth exactly as hard as Earth pulls the Sun. Teal arrows: velocities.
  • Trails show recent paths. Bodies are drawn larger than life, or the planets would be invisible dots (unless realistic physics is on).

The key idea

Newton's law of gravitation, F = Gm1m2/r2: twice the distance, a quarter of the pull. A planet is always falling towards the Sun, but it moves sideways so fast that it keeps missing. That gives Kepler's laws: orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focus (1st), a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times (2nd), and T2/a3 is the same for every planet of a star (3rd).

Realistic physics

Off, gravity is Newton's alone. On, every body is drawn at its true size (a ring marks the tiny ones); Einstein's general relativity adds its first correction to gravity, so an orbit close to a neutron star or black hole slowly turns, tracing a rosette (Mercury's turns too, by 43″ a century); and a star or planet that strays inside the Roche limit of a much denser body is torn apart by tides. Either way, bodies merge when their real surfaces touch and a black hole swallows anything that crosses its event horizon.

Things to try

  • Raise the eccentricity and turn on the 2nd law: the planet speeds up near the Sun but the shaded areas stay equal.
  • Drop more planets and turn on the 3rd law to compare their periods.
  • In "Slingshot", Jupiter only turns the probe, yet its speed relative to the Sun doubles.
  • Run "Three stars: chaos", then nudge one star and run it again.
  • Aim a velocity at the escape speed (it snaps there): the body only just gets away.

How to use it

Drag bodies from the strip into space, and drag bodies to move them. Drag the tip of a velocity arrow to aim it (hold Shift for fine control); it snaps to the exact circular and escape speeds. Right-click or long-press a body for its menu, where you can also set its speed when arrows are hidden; double-click or double-tap it to view the motion from it. Drag empty space to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom. Space pauses. opens the settings, goes fullscreen, shows energy graphs and restarts.
