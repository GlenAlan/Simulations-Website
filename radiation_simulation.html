About this simulation

Thermal radiation is energy carried by electromagnetic waves, mostly infrared, which we cannot see. It needs no particles, so it can cross empty space – that is how the Sun warms the Earth. Here a hot heater in a vacuum chamber radiates to two metal blocks.

What you are seeing:

  • Each wavy line is a packet of radiation, slowed down enormously. Each carries the same energy; infrared is drawn pink, visible light in its real colour.
  • A packet that reaches a surface is either absorbed (a small flash: its energy heats the object) or reflected.

Key ideas:

  • Everything emits radiation: P = εσAT4 (T in kelvin), so hotter objects emit far more, and at shorter wavelengths.
  • Good absorbers are good emitters: matte black absorbs 97% of incoming radiation; polished silver reflects 98%.
  • A block warms up until it emits as much as it absorbs. Radiation spreads out, so doubling the distance cuts the power received to a quarter.

Things to try:

  • Turn on Energy flow and compare the black and silver blocks. In a vacuum the silver one does reach the same final temperature – after hours, not minutes.
  • Try the Visible light only and Thermal camera views, or switch the heater off.

How to interact: drag a block sideways (mouse or finger) to move it. opens the settings, shows temperature and spectrum charts, restarts and goes fullscreen.

Model: a thermostat holds the 8 cm heater ball at its set temperature; each hollow 8 cm block warms at (power absorbed − power emitted) ÷ heat capacity. The chamber walls stay at 20 °C.
