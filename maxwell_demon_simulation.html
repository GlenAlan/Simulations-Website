About this simulation

In 1867 James Clerk Maxwell imagined a tiny "demon" guarding a door between two boxes of gas. By letting only fast molecules through one way and slow ones the other, it could make one side hot and the other cold without doing any work, apparently breaking the second law of thermodynamics.

What you're seeing

  • Gas particles collide elastically. Red ones are faster than the starting median speed, blue ones slower; collisions keep changing their speeds and colours.
  • The demon opens the gate only for fast particles going one way and slow ones going the other.
  • shows each chamber's temperature (average kinetic energy) relative to the start.

The key idea

Sorting by speed lowers the gas's entropy: one side warms and the other cools. But to sort, the demon has to measure each particle and remember the result. Erasing that memory costs at least kT ln 2 of energy per bit (Landauer's principle), which creates more entropy than the sorting removed, so the second law survives.

Things to try

  • Be the demon: choose Manual in the settings (), then press and hold on the simulation (mouse, touch or pen), or hold Space, to open the gate. Can you beat the automatic demon?
  • Try a wider gate, more particles or a higher speed.
