About this simulation

Nothing can go faster than light, and light's speed, c, is the same for everyone, however fast they move. Einstein realised that this forces time and space themselves to stretch and shrink.

What you're seeing

  • A ship and a station of the same length each carry a light clock: a flash of light bouncing between two mirrors that ticks once a second. To make that work the mirrors are half a light-second (150,000 km) apart, so lengths are in light-seconds (ls).
  • Everything is drawn as it is measured from one frame (the station's or the ship's), not as a camera would see it: the delay of light reaching an eye is left out.

Key idea

The moving clock's light has to zig-zag a longer path at the same speed c, so it ticks slower: t = γτ, where γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²). Moving objects are also shorter along their motion: L = L0/γ. At 0.6c, γ = 1.25; at 0.8c, 1.67.

Things to try

  • Switch to measured from the ship: now the station is short and its clock is slow. Both views are right; turn on the clocks at each end to see why (clocks synchronised in one frame aren't in the other).
  • Push the speed past 0.99c and watch the moving clock almost stop.
  • In the twin paradox the traveller comes home younger; in muons, real particles that should decay high in the sky reach the ground.

How to interact

Drag the speed slider and pick a frame under the simulation, or tap the ship or station to ride along with it. opens the settings (scenarios and extra details), goes fullscreen, restarts the clocks and shows graphs. Press F to show the frame rate.
