About this simulation

Light always travels at the same speed, c, for everyone, however fast they move. Einstein realised this means moving clocks run slow and moving objects shrink.

What you're seeing

  • A ship and a station of the same length, each with a light clock: light bouncing between two mirrors, one tick per round trip. The mirrors are half a light-second (150,000 km) apart, so each clock ticks once a second. Lengths are in light-seconds (ls).
  • Everything is as measured from one frame (the station's or the ship's), not as a camera would see it. The moving craft comes round again after leaving the screen.

Key idea

In the moving clock the light zig-zags further at the same speed, so it ticks slower: t = γτ, where τ is the time the moving clock shows, t the time measured in the frame and γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²). The moving craft is also shorter: L = L0/γ. At 0.6c, γ = 1.25; at 0.8c, 1.67.

Things to try

  • Measure from the ship: now the station is short and its clock slow. Both views are right. Turn on the clocks at each end to see why: clocks synchronised on one craft aren't, measured from the other.
  • Go past 0.99c and the moving clock almost stops.
  • In the twin paradox the traveller comes home younger; muons made high in the sky reach the ground only thanks to relativity.

How to interact

Drag the speed slider and pick a frame under the simulation, or tap the ship or station to ride along with it. opens the settings (scenarios and extras), goes fullscreen, restarts the clocks and shows graphs. Press F to show the frame rate.
