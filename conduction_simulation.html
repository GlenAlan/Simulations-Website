About this simulation

In a solid, heat is the jiggling of atoms. Here a 2D lattice of atoms is held together by spring-like bonds: a vibrating atom tugs on its neighbours, so the energy spreads from atom to atom. That spreading is conduction.

What you're seeing

  • Each dot is an atom. Its colour shows how strongly it vibrates: blue is cold, then green and yellow, and red is hot.
  • The lattice starts at rest, at absolute zero. The readout is the average vibrational energy per atom, on an illustrative kelvin scale.
  • When you are not heating or cooling, energy is conserved: the average stays the same while heat flows from hot regions to cold ones.

Things to try

  • Heat one end of the lattice and watch the warmth creep along it.
  • Raise Bond stiffness: stiffer bonds pass vibrations on faster, so heat conducts more quickly.
  • Heat a patch, then cool the atoms next to it and watch heat flow into the cold spot.

How to interact

Press and drag on the lattice to heat it, or pick Cool below the simulation to cool it instead. Shortcuts: right-click or Ctrl+click cools with a mouse, and two fingers cool on a touchscreen. opens the settings and goes fullscreen.
