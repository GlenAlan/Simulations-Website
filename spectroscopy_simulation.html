About this simulation

Stand under a night sky, pick out an object and read its light to work out what it is, how hot it is, what it is made of and how it is moving.

What you're seeing

  • A sky of 1,000 objects, from red dwarfs to quasars, each with a 6-character code and its own spectrum. Everything in it is fictional, apart from seven home planets in made-up places. No Sun, Moon or Earth appear.
  • Five bands, from radio to gamma rays. All but Visible are in false colour.

Key idea

Light carries a record of what made it. Its peak gives the temperature, λmax = b/T. Dark or bright lines name the elements, their shift gives the speed, v = cz, and their width gives spin and density.

Things to try

  • Find a hot star's peak in the ultraviolet and apply Wien's law.
  • Measure the redshifts of galaxies and plot Hubble's law.
  • Plot a whole cluster on the H-R diagram and read its age.
  • Help lists twelve tasks for Modules 5, 7 and 8, ending in a search of the whole sky.

How to interact

Drag to look, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click an object to open its panel. Press / to go to a code. opens the settings, goes fullscreen, and Help lists every key.

Line data: NIST Atomic Spectra Database. Drawn with three.js.
